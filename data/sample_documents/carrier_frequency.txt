Document: Carrier Frequency — Characterization Notes
Version: Illustrative v1.0

─────────────────────────────────────────────
1. PARAMETER DEFINITION
─────────────────────────────────────────────
The NFC carrier frequency is nominally 13.56 MHz, as mandated by ISO/IEC 14443-2. This frequency lies in the globally allocated ISM (Industrial, Scientific, and Medical) band at 13.553–13.567 MHz. The standard allows for a frequency tolerance of ±7 kHz around the nominal value, though tighter product specifications may apply for specific product families.

─────────────────────────────────────────────
2. WHY IT MATTERS
─────────────────────────────────────────────
The tag IC contains a demodulator circuit tuned to detect the 13.56 MHz carrier. If the reader's frequency deviates significantly, the demodulator's ability to reliably recover the data clock degrades. This results in higher bit-error rates and potentially failed transactions.

The tag antenna is also designed as a resonant circuit tuned close to 13.56 MHz. A mismatch between the antenna resonance frequency and the actual carrier frequency reduces power transfer efficiency, potentially preventing the IC from powering up at all in weak-field conditions.

─────────────────────────────────────────────
3. MEASUREMENT METHOD (ILLUSTRATIVE)
─────────────────────────────────────────────
In a characterization environment, the carrier frequency is measured at the output of the signal generator connected to the reference reader loop. A frequency counter or spectrum analyser is used to verify the actual frequency with sub-kHz precision.

Environmental factors such as temperature drift in the oscillator crystal can shift the frequency slightly. At cold temperatures (e.g. -40°C), the frequency may shift up; at hot temperatures (+85°C), it may shift down, depending on the crystal's frequency-temperature characteristic.

4. TYPICAL SPECIFICATION (SYNTHETIC ILLUSTRATIVE VALUES)
─────────────────────────────────────────────
Nominal:  13.56 MHz
LSL:      13.50 MHz  (lower specification limit)

These values are entirely illustrative. They are not drawn from any real product specification sheet.

─────────────────────────────────────────────
5. COMMON CAUSES OF OUT-OF-SPEC READINGS
─────────────────────────────────────────────
- Oscillator crystal ageing or damage during handling
- Test equipment calibration drift
- Electromagnetic interference in the test environment
- Incorrect test setup (e.g., cable length affecting impedance)
- Temperature not stabilised before measurement
