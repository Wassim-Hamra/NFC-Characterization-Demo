1. STANDARD TEST CONDITIONS
─────────────────────────────────────────────
Semiconductor characterization tests are performed under controlled conditions to ensure reproducibility and comparability across lots and sites. The standard measurement conditions for NFC IC testing (illustrative) are:

Temperature: 25°C (room temperature, also called "nominal" or "TYP" corner)
Supply Voltage: 3.3 V (nominal supply voltage for the illustrated IC family)
Humidity: Controlled, typically 40-60% RH
Settling time: IC must be powered and stable for at least 1 ms before measurement begins

─────────────────────────────────────────────
2. CORNER TESTING
─────────────────────────────────────────────
Corner testing verifies that the IC meets its specifications not only at nominal conditions but also at the extremes of its operating range.

TEMPERATURE CORNERS (ILLUSTRATIVE):
- Cold corner:  -40°C (automotive applications) or 0°C (consumer/industrial)
- Hot corner:   +85°C (industrial) or +125°C (automotive)
- Nominal:       25°C

VOLTAGE CORNERS (ILLUSTRATIVE):
- Low voltage:  3.0 V (Vdd_min)
- Nominal:      3.3 V
- High voltage: 3.6 V (Vdd_max)

COMBINED CORNERS:
Full corner testing combines temperature and voltage extremes:
- Best case:  low temperature + high voltage (fastest transistors, most current)
- Worst case: high temperature + low voltage (slowest transistors, least margin)

4. IMPACT OF TEMPERATURE ON MEASUREMENTS
─────────────────────────────────────────────
Most NFC IC parameters show temperature dependence:

Carrier Frequency: Crystal oscillator frequency shifts with temperature. The frequency-temperature characteristic (FTC) of a quartz crystal is typically a parabola with its peak near room temperature.

Load Modulation: The on-resistance of the modulation switch increases with temperature (for NMOS devices), which can slightly reduce the modulation amplitude at hot corners.

Receiver Sensitivity: Thermal noise power increases with temperature (kTB), slightly degrading sensitivity at hot corners.

Modulation Depth: May shift with temperature due to changes in transistor threshold voltages and saturation characteristics.

─────────────────────────────────────────────
5. TEST SITE AND HANDLER EFFECTS
─────────────────────────────────────────────
In high-volume manufacturing, chips are tested on automated test equipment (ATE) using chip handlers. Each test site (prober head or contactor nest) can introduce systematic offset due to:
- Contact resistance variation
- Parasitics in the probe card
- Local thermal gradients
- Calibration differences between handler sites

Good practice includes comparing measurement distributions across test sites within the same lot. A systematic offset between sites indicates a handler-related systematic error rather than a true device variation.
