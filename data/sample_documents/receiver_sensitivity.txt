Document: Receiver Sensitivity — Characterization Notes
Version: Illustrative v1.0

─────────────────────────────────────────────
1. PARAMETER DEFINITION
─────────────────────────────────────────────
Receiver sensitivity is the minimum RF input power level at which an NFC tag IC can correctly receive and decode a command from the reader. It is expressed in dBm (decibels relative to one milliwatt). A more negative dBm value indicates better (more sensitive) reception.

For example, a sensitivity of -60 dBm means the tag can operate at an input power 60 dB below 1 mW, which is equivalent to 1 nanowatt (1 nW).

─────────────────────────────────────────────
2. WHY IT MATTERS
─────────────────────────────────────────────
In a real NFC system, the available power at the tag antenna decreases rapidly with distance (following a 1/r³ relationship in the near-field regime). A tag with poor receiver sensitivity will fail to operate at the distances specified for the application, leading to customer-visible read failures.

Receiver sensitivity also interacts with the tag's power harvesting capability. A tag that powers up at a certain field level but cannot reliably decode data until a stronger field is applied provides a degraded user experience.

─────────────────────────────────────────────
3. MEASUREMENT METHOD (ILLUSTRATIVE)
─────────────────────────────────────────────
In a characterization bench, receiver sensitivity is measured by gradually reducing the reader field strength (expressed as H-field in A/m, or equivalently as power at the reader antenna port) until the tag stops responding correctly. The last power level at which the tag correctly returns a valid response is recorded as the sensitivity measurement.

ISO 14443 defines specific measurement coil geometries and test conditions to ensure reproducibility across different test setups.

─────────────────────────────────────────────
4. TYPICAL SPECIFICATION (SYNTHETIC ILLUSTRATIVE VALUES)
─────────────────────────────────────────────
Nominal: -60 dBm
LSL:     -65 dBm  (better than -65 is acceptable; more negative = more sensitive)
USL:     -50 dBm  (sensitivity worse than -50 dBm fails)

Note: For sensitivity parameters, the sign convention must be tracked carefully. A measurement of -63 dBm is better than -50 dBm. The LSL and USL refer to the numeric value, not the physical sensitivity.

─────────────────────────────────────────────
5. SYSTEMATIC SHIFT — INVESTIGATION GUIDE
─────────────────────────────────────────────
A systematic upward shift in receiver sensitivity (e.g., from -60 dBm toward -50 dBm) across an entire production lot is a serious finding. It means chips are becoming less sensitive on average. Possible causes:

- LNA (low-noise amplifier) gain degradation due to process variation
- Excessive leakage or mismatch in the RF front-end transistors
- Antenna quality factor (Q) reduction due to resistive losses
- Incorrect bias point in the RF input stage
- Test equipment calibration offset (systematic error affecting the entire lot's measurements)

A systematic shift of 3-5 dBm across a lot, with the distribution remaining otherwise Gaussian, is a strong indicator of a process-level shift rather than random variation or test equipment noise.

If the shift is confirmed in independent re-test with calibrated equipment, the root cause investigation should focus on:
1. Process control charts for the implant, gate oxide, and BEOL steps for the affected wafers
2. Comparison with the electrical parametric monitor (EPM) data for the same wafers
3. Die-level spatial maps to identify any within-wafer gradient
